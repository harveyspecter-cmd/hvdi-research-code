How the scores work

Each of five measures runs from 0 to 4: claimed, technical proof, adoption, capital quality and buyer economics. A score of 3 or more needs proof from a buyer, an independent tracker or a filing.

Proof value V is the weighted mean of technical proof, adoption and buyer economics. Gap = claimed minus V. +1.0 or more: story ahead. −1.0 or less: proof ahead.

Capital quality is scored but kept out of the gap. Gates sit outside the arithmetic; a blocked gate overrides the outlook.

Curve position: gap of +1 or more with V under 1.5 is "promise outruns proof"; with V of 1.5 or more, "peak claims"; a negative gap is "proof outruns talk"; otherwise V under 3 is "earning it" and 3 or more is "earned". These are this study's positions, not Gartner's.

Outlook is an uncalibrated rule: +1 for a dated milestone aimed at the weakest proof, ±1 for whether the last milestone was on time, ±1 for capital quality.

Limits

Project analysis assigned every score; blind AI codings and five blind human coders reproduced all ten verdicts, though the human coders read claims more cautiously. A reading at one date describes timing risk, not eventual success. An exploratory back-test on 400G Ethernet and OpenFlow suggested that the gap's direction over time separates the two, and identified refinements for a version 3.2, which is separate from this project.

Any pre-production technology shows a positive gap; that marks timing risk, not a bad technology. Buyer economics are mostly private, so nothing scores above 2 there.

Several operator results came through second-hand reports. Scenarios test exposure and predict nothing. Not investment advice.
